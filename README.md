## Campaign brief

| Field | Complete before launch |
| --- | --- |
| Business problem | What needs to change and why? |
| Objective | Which action, target, and time period? |
| Audience | Who needs the offer and what problem do they have? |
| Offer | What is being offered, with what conditions? |
| Core message | One clear benefit supported by evidence |
| Channel | Where will the audience encounter the message? |
| Landing destination | The page or form and its next step |
| Budget and currency | Approved total, dates, and allocation |
| Primary metric | The measure closest to the objective |
| Supporting metrics | Measures that explain performance |
| Tracking | Naming conventions, events, and reporting source |
| Owner | Who prepares, checks, approves, and reports? |
| Review date | When will the team decide what to change? |

### Launch check

- [ ] The city, venue, date, offer, and call to action agree everywhere.
- [ ] Each claim is accurate and can be supported.
- [ ] The page works on mobile and the main action is easy to find.
- [ ] The form asks for the information needed for the stated purpose.
- [ ] Confirmation and follow-up steps are ready.
- [ ] Tracking and naming have been checked.
- [ ] Budget, dates, and publication approval are confirmed.

Give each item a purpose. Keep the hook, useful information, proof, and next step connected. Review results against the objective rather than treating every reaction as a business outcome.

## Website review checklist

### Message and journey

- [ ] The first screen explains the offer and who it helps.
- [ ] Headings describe the content clearly.
- [ ] The main call to action matches the visitor's next step.
- [ ] Navigation and links work on mobile and desktop.
- [ ] Product or service information answers common questions.

### Usability and content

- [ ] Text is readable and controls have useful labels.
- [ ] Informative images have meaningful alternative text.
- [ ] Forms provide clear instructions and error messages.
- [ ] Essential information is accessible without relying only on colour.
- [ ] Images are appropriately sized and the page loads acceptably.

### Measurement

- [ ] The purpose of each tracked event is documented.
- [ ] Test submissions are distinguishable from real enquiries.
- [ ] Reporting periods and filters are recorded.
- [ ] A proposed change has a specific hypothesis and success measure.

## Measurement guide

| Metric | Calculation | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Click-through rate | Clicks / impressions × 100 | Use the same click definition throughout |
| Cost per click | Spend / clicks | Does not establish lead quality |
| Cost per valid lead | Spend / valid leads | Define valid and remove duplicates consistently |
| Landing-page conversion rate | Conversions / eligible visits × 100 | State the visit and conversion definitions |
| Attendance rate | Attendees / confirmed registrations × 100 | Align the event and registration cohort |
| Return on ad spend | Attributed revenue / ad spend | Revenue is not profit; attribution has limits |

For zero denominators, report the metric as not available rather than zero. Record currency, timezone, date range, attribution window, and exclusions beside the results.

### Worked example with fictional data

A demonstration campaign spends EUR 120, receives 200 clicks from 10,000 impressions, and generates 20 valid leads.

- Click-through rate: 200 / 10,000 × 100 = **2%**.
- Cost per click: 120 / 200 = **EUR 0.60**.
- Cost per valid lead: 120 / 20 = **EUR 6.00**.

These are fictional numbers for explaining calculations. They do not represent my campaign results, and they do not establish profitability.

## Adult learning lesson plan

| Field | Planning prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Learner context | What do learners already know and use at work? |
| Learning outcome | What should they be able to do after this lesson? |
| Explanation | What is the simplest accurate explanation? |
| Demonstration | Which practical task will make it visible? |
| Guided practice | What will learners do with support? |
| Independent task | What can they complete on their own? |
| Assessment | What evidence shows they can do it? |
| Feedback | What worked and what is the next useful correction? |
| Reflection | What should be clarified in the next lesson? |

### Example outcome

Instead of only planning to cover email marketing, define an action: by the end of the session, the learner can draft an email with a clear audience, subject line, useful message, and one call to action.

## Improvement log

For each revision, record the date, the problem found, the change made, and the reason. Keep examples distinct from real business results.
